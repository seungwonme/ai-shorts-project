# Visual distribution (flow B)

Read when building the visual distribution table and the picture pieces of a flow B spec, or when a render stops on a picture rule. (Routing and invariants live in `../SKILL.md`; this file is the detail.)

Never show one picture for the whole short. Give each script line a screen that matches what the line says, about 2-4 seconds per screen. The main way is the **real article page**: the capture `page.png` with a red box on the sentence the narration is reading and the rest of the page darkened, moving down the page as the narration goes on.

- Allowed material: the page capture (`page.png`, from `capture-article.ps1`), an article photo (`img-NN`) and its region crops, and a web photo that passed the rights check (`web-NN`) **and is directly related to its line**. Never invent or generate anything. Do not type article text into a picture yourself: show the real page.
- **No filler photos.** A photo that is not directly about what its line says (a street sign, a drum, a skyline for an academic symposium) is never used, even if it is free and checked. When the screens run short, use these solutions in this order:
    1. **The article page capture + sentence highlight** (`type: "highlight"` on `재료/기사-<id>/page.png` with `sentence` or `quote`, see the page-capture rule below). Different sentences are different screens, so a page with 20+ sentences gives plenty.
    2. Different parts of an article image (`crop`, one meaningful region each, for example the title block, host logos, the date line; measured per image, see below), with or without a `highlight` box.
        - When the article has images (`img-NN.jpg`), show one of them again once or twice in the middle of the video to break the monotony of body text: `{"type": "image", "src": "재료/기사-<id>/img-NN.jpg", "crop": "named:<name>"}` with a different region each time, at a line that talks about what the picture shows. Not two picture screens in a row, and the same region at most twice.
        - **Crops are measured per image, never copied from another article or from an example.** Run (from the project folder) `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .agents/skills/kaist-shorts-make/scripts/measure-image.ps1 -Image "재료/기사-<id>/img-NN.jpg" -Write`. It measures the picture (size and the blocks of text or logos), proposes crops that keep letters whole and the important blocks in the upper 75% of the area (above the caption zone), checks them the same way `render.ps1` does, and writes the passing ones to `재료/기사-<id>/crops.json` (data of that article; `part-N` = larger groups of blocks, `detail-N` = single blocks). Look at the picture, rename the entries to meaningful names if you like (`title`, `date`), then use `"crop": "named:<name>"`. A name that is not in that picture's `crops.json` stops the render with the names that exist.
        - What the tool says about the picture decides what you do: for a **photo** (people, scenery: edges everywhere) it proposes nothing, and you use the photo without a crop (the center fills the area) or write `crop` numbers after looking at it; for a landscape picture nothing is needed either. Numbers you type must come from this picture: do not reuse the crop of another picture.
        - Do not invent narrower crops than the tool proposes: a crop narrower than about 70% of the picture width zooms in, may cut letters at the sides and pushes text under the caption. The QR code or address strip at the very bottom of a tall poster often cannot be lifted above the caption zone (the tool marks such a block as not croppable): leave it out.
        - `render.ps1` **stops** on `image` pieces of tall pictures (height at least 1.1 times the width; not on video pieces, not on landscape photos) when, for a text-poster-like crop, the lower 25% of the screen has about as much text or logo as the upper 75% ("자막 영역에 글자나 로고가 몰려 있어 자막이 덮습니다") or when letters touch the left or right edge of the crop ("크롭의 좌우 가장자리에 글자나 그림이 걸쳐 있어 잘렸습니다"; an edge where the crop reaches the picture's own border is not counted). **These two checks are heuristics** (edge counts at 216x151, thresholds named `$PIC_*` at the top of `render.ps1`, checked on a small set of article pictures only). A crop that looks right to you but is stopped: first adjust the crop; if the stop is clearly wrong, add `"allow": {"caption_text": "<reason>"}` or `{"edge_cut": "<reason>"}` to that piece (the reason is required, at least 4 characters, and is printed in the render output; tell the user which check you skipped and why). Never use `allow` to hide a real overlap.
    3. A photo that is directly about the line (Openverse or Commons, with approval and rights check).
    - `text_card` (a white card with sentences copied from `article.md`, see the page-capture rule below) only when the user has said it may replace the page capture. A failed capture is never a reason by itself: stop and ask (see Flow B step 1).
- Before the approval message, build the table (template item 4): 줄 번호 / 자료 / 영역 / 비율 / 움직임 / 움직임 이유 / 길이, where the 영역 cell names the highlighted sentence (number and first words) for every page-capture row. Measure each poster or photo first (`ffmpeg -hide_banner -i <file>` prints `WxH`) and write a crop's real pixel ratio in the 비율 column (`-` for page-capture rows: the renderer makes the crop). Check each row against the line it carries.
- Rules that `render.ps1` enforces (it stops with a Korean reason, there is no way around it):
    - Different screens >= ceil(pieces / 2). A screen is a different (image, crop, box). A different sentence box on the same image is a different screen. The same screen at most 2 times in a video, never twice in a row (3 or more times stops the render). Crops or boxes of the same image that overlap by half or more (intersection over union 0.5) count as the same screen, so sliding a window by a few percent is not a new screen.
    - A piece longer than 4 seconds in narration mode: split it into 2 screens that differ. **The best split is two different sentences** (the one the line is reading plus the next sentence of the same paragraph, or a poster crop). The same sentence number counts as the same screen; it may be used twice in a row only when `lines` ranges that do not overlap split a long sentence (for example `lines [1,2]` and `lines [3,4]` of a 4-line sentence).
    - Short boxes: when `lines` cuts a sentence, the box must hold at least 40% of the sentence characters, at least 5 characters per line, and a single-line box must be at least 60% of the longest line. Otherwise it stops with "문장을 줄 단위로 나누면 박스가 너무 짧아집니다" (a box around only "있다." or "논의한다." is useless). Then show the whole sentence on one screen, or go to another sentence. Never cut off one last line.
    - Visual material between the text (`render.ps1` stops when the article has an image and 4 or more page-capture `highlight` pieces follow each other): **at most 3 body-sentence `highlight` pieces in a row, then an image piece** (the whole lead image, a different measured part of it, or another article photo). Use different parts of the poster as separate screens (title, host-logo rows, date and place, keywords, QR and address; crop from `img-01.jpg`, the original has more detail than `page.png`) and a `highlight` box on a part when it helps (for example a box on the date). Alternate: poster, 3 sentences, poster part, 3 sentences, poster part ... Never only red-box text; the user finds that monotonous.
    - Warning only: if the article has an image and the first piece uses `page.png` but is not that lead image, `render.ps1` prints "경고: 첫 조각이 기사의 대표 이미지(맨 위 그림)가 아닙니다". Fix it before the user sees the video.
    - Black bars: `"fit": "width"` may leave at most 15% of the area empty. A whole portrait picture with `fit: width` leaves a large black area (a 4:5 poster leaves 44%), so crop a region (ratio 1.0:1-2.0:1, closest to 1.43:1) instead. Thin strips wider than 3:1 print a warning.
    - Boxes: a `box` outside the 1080x756 area, or one that points at an almost empty part of the picture (no text or picture inside), stops the render. A `text_card` that needs more than 7 lines or has a word wider than the card also stops it.
    - Photo reason: every `web-NN` photo in the spec needs `used_for` in `sources.json` (which line, what it shows, one line). Empty: "이 사진을 왜 쓰는지 적어야 합니다".
- Motion: screens are still by default (no `push_in`). A `push_in` piece (0.04-0.08, only on a plain `image` piece) needs a reason in the 움직임 이유 column. `highlight` and `text_card` never move; only the box fades in (starts 0.3 s after the piece starts, 0.3 s long; the outside darkens together, about 58% black).
- Captions: **`inside` (the default) is final**: white text with a black outline in the bottom of the video area. Do not set `subtitle_position` for normal shorts. (`bar` = a black translucent strip behind the caption, and `below` = under the bottom note y about 1540 which is the empty area the template keeps free for the YouTube Shorts buttons, are options the user may choose; never recommend them by yourself.) The overlap with a page capture is solved by the **box position**, not by the caption: the bottom 25% of the video area (the caption zone; the caption is one line of 40 px about 36 px above the bottom) stays free of boxes. For a page-capture sentence the renderer centers the sentence at about 35% of the area height (the upper 70%). A box (any `highlight`, also on a poster part) that reaches into the lower 25% stops the render with "자막과 겹칩니다. 문장을 위로 올리세요"; for your own `crop` + `box` keep the box in the upper 75%. Plain `image` pieces have no box to check: put the important part of the crop in the upper 75% too. The last sentences of the page (the end of the article) cannot be raised because the page ends; close the short with a poster piece (for example the date and place, or the QR and address) instead of a highlight on them.
- **First piece**: when the article has a usable lead image (`page-boxes.json` lists images and the first is not wider than 2.4:1), start with it: `{"type": "image", "src": "재료/기사-<id>/page.png", "crop": "poster", "duration": 2.7}` (`"lead"` is an alias; the crop takes the top of the first article image with 7% side margins and is a layout rule, not tuned to one picture). `render.ps1` warns when a `page.png` first piece is not that image. If it stops the piece, use the lead image file with a measured `named:` crop as the first piece. With no usable lead image start with the first sentence `highlight`. Per-variant worked references: `example-article-poster-69410.md`, `example-article-photos.md`, `example-article-no-image.md`.
- Faces: keep the 초상권 notice. Never crop to isolate, distort or mock a face; prefer text.
- **Page capture + `highlight`** (the normal way): `{"type": "highlight", "src": "재료/기사-<id>/page.png", "sentence": 4, "duration": 2.8}`. `sentence` is the sentence number from `page-boxes.json` (1 = the first sentence of the body; a list like `[4, 5]` highlights both). Or `"quote": "<the first words of the sentence>"` (the first words of the sentence, as written in `page-boxes.json`; it must match exactly one sentence). Open `page-boxes.json` and read the sentence list with numbers before you choose; it is the only place where the numbers are defined. The renderer takes the position from `page-boxes.json` (never write `box` numbers for page.png), crops the page to the 1080:756 area with the sentence in the middle at full width (`"crop"` may be left out or `"auto"`), draws the red box around the sentence and darkens the rest. If the sentence is too long for one readable screen (stops with a Korean message), split it with `"lines": [1, 3]` (the first piece) and `"lines": [4, 6]` (the second piece): only those lines of the sentence are boxed. If the text would be too small a warning is printed. Move the box down the page in reading order; the same sentence at most twice.
- `crop`: `"crop": {"x": 0.1, "y": 0.1, "w": 0.8, "h": 0.45}` (fractions 0-1 of the source) on an `image` or `highlight` piece. The region fills the 1080x756 area (sides or top and bottom are trimmed when the ratio differs); `"fit": "width"` fits the whole region instead (black bars, see the 15% rule).
- `highlight` on a poster or photo: `{"type": "highlight", "src": "재료/기사-<id>/img-NN.jpg", "crop": {...}, "box": {"x": 0.27, "y": 0.11, "w": 0.46, "h": 0.17}, "duration": 2.6}`. `box` is in fractions of the reference image (the crop region if there is a `crop`, else the whole image). Do not guess the box from memory: look at the picture and measure the text block; a box on an empty part stops the render.
- `text_card` (emergency only): `{"type": "text_card", "lines": ["<sentence copied from article.md>"], "highlight": [1], "duration": 3.4}`. A white card with the sentences in Gmarket Sans 44 px, at most 7 wrapped lines; `highlight` is the sentence number in `lines`. Not for normal use.
- Credit: when a `video` segment comes from a folder that has `meta.json` (made by `collect-video.ps1`), `render.ps1` writes one line per source video into `credits.txt` ("원본 영상: \"title\" - channel, address, 라이선스: ..."). Give it to the user for the video description; the original must be credited even when its license is not CC BY.
- Credit: when `page.png` is used, `render.ps1` writes `credits.txt` next to `shorts.mp4` with the source line ("기사 화면 캡처: 출처: KAIST 뉴스, <address>, 캡처 <date>"); `capture-article.ps1` records the source and the capture time in `sources.json` (`page_capture`). Give the text to the user for the video description.
- Examples (format references only; their `재료/기사-69410/...` paths are sample data of one article, and the named crops in `기사캡처.spec.json` need `examples/crops-69410.example.json` copied to `재료/기사-69410/crops.json`): `examples/포스터-크롭.spec.json` (crops), `examples/하이라이트.spec.json` (poster highlight), `examples/기사캡처.spec.json` (page capture + sentences).
